The Broken Bot Labyrinth

Team codeX: Wendy Calma, Harman Magar, Justine Esguerra Topic: Conversational UX and dialogue design Result: All 6 levels cleared, Final Boss defeated

We had to repair a broken campus help bot. Each level gave a scenario and a list of gate criteria, which were the expected results, and ChatGPT tested our "repair prompt" against them. We started with a 1,200-token budget, so long prompts cost us.

A failed gate told us exactly which criterion we missed, so we changed only that part.

Level 1, attempt 1 (failed) 
REPAIR PROMPT: "I'm sorry you're having trouble accessing MyClass. Are you seeing a specific error message, or is the page not loading for you?"

✗ Didn't separate forgotten credentials or an outage affecting others.

Level 1, attempt 2 (cleared) 
REPAIR PROMPT: "I'm sorry you're having trouble accessing MyClass. To help me assist you, are you seeing an error message, is the page not loading for everyone, or are you having trouble with your login credentials?"

✓ All five gate criteria met.

Final Boss: one prompt for every situation

CCS Help Assistant Behavioral Directive:
1. INTENT & CONTEXT: Triage user queries into credential loss, system errors, or outages by verifying impact scope (individual device vs. multi-user).
2. WORKFLOW & DECISION: Limit interactions to a strict six-turn limit. Clarify ambiguities immediately; escalate multi-user issues to specialized support without delay. Confirmation: For consequential requests, state exactly what will be submitted and ask for clear Yes/No confirmation.
3. RECOVERY: If communication fails, execute a four-stage ladder: rephrase, narrow options, take control, and escalate.
TRUST: Address accessibility barriers without simulating human emotion. For login difficulties, prioritize links to Disability Services and official support channels.

The Boss ran five hidden tests: vague intent, a scope branch, a shared password, a corrected device and a request to "mark it resolved."

Our first attempt passed 9 of 10 checks. It never required confirmation before a consequential submission, so we added the Confirmation sentence above. The second attempt passed all 10.

What I took from it: a behavior I fixed in Level 4 didn't carry into the master prompt by itself. I had to write it in again.
